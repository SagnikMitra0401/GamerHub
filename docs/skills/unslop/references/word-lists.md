# Word lists (German and English)

Level 1 of the skill. A hit is a reason to look at the sentence, not a verdict on the text.

## Tiers

**Tier 1: the phrase is the tell.** Stock formulas that a careful writer almost never reaches for. One hit in a short text is worth a fix; three in one page are a finding.

**Tier 2: context decides.** Words that carry real meaning in some registers and inflate every claim in others. „umfassend“ is accurate in a legal scope clause and empty in a product page. Flag only where the word replaces a fact.

**Tier 3: weak alone.** Ordinary words that models overuse. Never a finding on their own. They add weight only when they cluster with Tier 1 or Tier 2 hits in the same passage.

## Thresholds

- Tier 1: one hit per paragraph is enough to rewrite that sentence. Three or more in a page is P1 as a cluster.
- Tier 2: flag where a number, a name or a mechanism could stand in place of the adjective.
- Tier 3: ignore unless five or more hits across two tiers cluster with uniform sentence rhythm. That cluster justifies a structural review, never an authorship claim.

## German, Tier 1

Openers and scaffolding: „In der heutigen digitalen Welt“, „In der heutigen schnelllebigen Zeit“, „Im Zeitalter von“, „Lass uns einen Blick werfen“, „Tauchen wir ein in“, „Begib dich auf eine Reise“, „Im Folgenden erfährst du“.

Empty intensifiers and verdicts: „ein wahres Meisterwerk“, „ein Muss für alle“, „spielt eine entscheidende Rolle“, „ist nicht wegzudenken“, „auf ein neues Level heben“, „einzigartige Kombination“, „die perfekte Lösung“.

Formulas: „nicht nur …, sondern auch“, „es ist wichtig zu beachten“, „es sei darauf hingewiesen“, „letztendlich kommt es darauf an“, „Fazit: “, „Zusammenfassend lässt sich sagen“.

Inflated change verbs: „revolutionieren“, „läuten eine neue Ära ein“, „markiert einen Wendepunkt“, „setzt neue Maßstäbe“, „verändert die Art und Weise, wie wir …“.

## German, Tier 2

Adjectives: „umfassend“, „ganzheitlich“, „nahtlos“, „mühelos“, „zukunftsweisend“, „wegweisend“, „leistungsstark“, „modernste“, „vielfältig“, „bemerkenswert“, „spannend“ (where nothing is exciting), „wertvoll“ (where no value is named).

Connectors used as filler: „Darüber hinaus“, „Zudem“, „Des Weiteren“, „Ferner“, „Abschließend“, „Nichtsdestotrotz“.

Nominal style in prose: „die Durchführung“, „die Umsetzung“ (as the subject of a sentence that hid its actor), „im Rahmen von“, „im Hinblick auf“, „in Bezug auf“, „hinsichtlich“.

Business abstractions: „Synergie“, „Mehrwert“ (unnamed), „Lösungsansatz“, „ganzheitlicher Ansatz“, „auf Augenhöhe“ (as a claimed virtue).

## German, Tier 3

„wichtig“, „entscheidend“, „zahlreiche“, „verschiedene“, „vielfältig“ (second use), „optimieren“, „effizient“, „innovativ“, „dynamisch“, „robust“ (outside engineering), „skalierbar“ (outside infrastructure), „Es gibt“.

## English, Tier 1

Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of", "Let's dive in", "Let's unpack", "Picture this", "Imagine a world where".

Formulas: "It's important to note", "It's worth mentioning", "not only … but also", "at the end of the day", "the bottom line is", "In conclusion", "All in all".

Verdicts and inflations: "a testament to", "a game-changer", "unlock the power of", "elevate your", "takes X to the next level", "the ultimate guide", "a must-have", "plays a crucial role", "stands as a".

## English, Tier 2

"delve into", "leverage", "utilize", "seamless", "robust" (outside engineering), "comprehensive", "holistic", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "streamline", "empower", "foster", "harness", "navigate the landscape", "in the realm of", "myriad", "plethora", "tapestry", "realm".

Connectors: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "That said", "Ultimately".

## English, Tier 3

"important", "crucial", "pivotal", "significant", "various", "ensure", "enhance", "optimize", "valuable", "insightful".

## Protected uses

These stay in place:

- Technical meaning: "robust" for a parser or a statistical estimator, "seamless" for a protocol handover, "leverage" in finance, "cache", "atomic", "idempotent".
- Legal, medical, safety and security text: "shall", "may", "must not", „darf nicht“, „vorbehaltlich“, „niemals“. Load-bearing language is content. Never soften or strengthen it.
- Brand and product names: a company that calls its product „Nahtlos“ keeps the name. In check mode, note the collision instead of flagging it.
- Quoted, attributed and literal uses: „Er tauchte in das Archiv ein“ is diving, not slop. "The delved plot" in an archaeology paper is a trench.
- Everyday words doing real work: „wichtig“ before a consequence the next sentence delivers.

## Microformats: German

- Percent: „50 %“ with a space, never „50%“. In ranges: „50–60 %“.
- Decimal comma, point for thousands: „3,5“, „1.200“, „12.500,00“.
- Currency after the amount: „40 €“, „1.200,00 €“.
- Dates: „24. November 2026“ in prose, „24.11.2026“ in tables and forms. Never „November 24, 2026“ inside German text.
- Time: „14:30 Uhr“.
- Ranges with numbers: en dash, no spaces: „S. 12–18“, „2024–2026“. With words: „von Montag bis Freitag“.
- Quotation marks: „so“, ‚so‘ inside a quotation.
- Dash: the Gedankenstrich is an en dash with spaces („ – “). An em dash without spaces inside German text is a double tell: it is both the wrong character and an English habit.
- Units: a space between number and unit („12 kg“, „230 V“), except degree signs („18 °C“ takes a space, „18°“ alone does not appear).

## Microformats: English

- "50%" without a space, "1,200.00", "November 24, 2026", "$1,200".
- Do not mix German and English number formats in one document. Mixed formats are a P1 finding in the skill's severity list.
